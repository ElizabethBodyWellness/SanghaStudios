How they're made

Natural and reclaimed fabric, cut into panels, sewn into a bell and finished by hand.

  1. Finding the fabric

    Camp blankets, wool, linen, knits and shag. Much of it is reclaimed, so each hat starts from a piece of fabric with its own pattern and history.

  2. Cutting the panels

    The pattern is laid out so the best part of the fabric lands on the front. The panels shape the bell and the brim, scalloped, flared or pointed.

  3. Sewing and stitching

    The panels are sewn together, the seams topstitched, and many hats get hand embroidery: flowers, animals, eyes, names.

  4. The loop

    Last comes the loop on top, so the hat can hang on a hook to dry between rounds in the heat.

Why a hat in the heat?

Heat rises, and your head is the first thing it finds. A good hat resists the flow of heat to your head and ears.

  • Ceiling
    hottest air pools here
  • Top bench
    where your head sits
  • Lower bench
    gentler
  • Floor
    often much cooler

It keeps your head cooler

Wool insulates. Your head stays comfortable while the rest of you takes the heat, so you can sit longer.

It protects hair and ears

Dry, very hot air is hard on hair and on the thin skin of the ears. A wool hat takes the brunt of it.

Caring for it

Hang it by its loop to air dry after each session. Every fabric is different, so ask Elizabeth how to wash yours.
