How they're made

Hot water, soap, and a lot of rolling. No glue, no seams.

  1. Choosing the wool

    Loose fleece gets sorted by color and fiber. Finer wool felts tighter; coarser wool holds a stiffer shape.

  2. Wet felting

    Thin layers are laid out, soaked in hot soapy water, and rubbed and rolled until the fibers lock into one sheet of cloth.

  3. Shaping on the block

    The damp felt is stretched over a hat block and worked into its final shape, point, or brim.

  4. Drying and finishing

    Each hat dries slowly on its block, then gets trimmed, brushed, and checked by hand.

Why a hat in the heat?

Heat rises. Your head is the first thing it finds.

  • Ceiling
    hottest air pools here
  • Top bench
    where your head sits
  • Lower bench
    gentler
  • Floor
    often much cooler

It keeps your head cooler

Wool felt insulates. Your head stays comfortable while the rest of you takes the heat, so you can sit longer.

It protects hair and ears

Dry, very hot air is hard on hair and on the thin skin of the ears. A felt hat takes the brunt of it.

Caring for it

Rinse in cool water now and then, press it back into shape, and let it air dry. Keep it out of the dryer.
